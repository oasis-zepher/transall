## Design Context

### Users
Local macOS users who handle research papers, Office files, Markdown notes, and PDFs. They need a private, repeatable document workbench for conversion, PDF cleanup, and PDF translation without sending files to a public web app by default.

### Brand Personality
Quiet, precise, utilitarian. The interface should feel like a professional local tool: dense enough for repeated use, clear enough for one-off conversions, and explicit about failure modes.

### Aesthetic Direction
Light-first, document-workbench style. Use restrained neutrals, precise borders, compact controls, and PDF thumbnails as the primary visual material. Avoid marketing hero layouts, decorative gradients, oversized cards, and vague onboarding copy.

### Design Principles
- Put the workflow on the first screen: upload, choose operation, run.
- Treat errors as part of the product: show logs and exact failed steps.
- Keep files and secrets local by default.
- Prefer compact, scannable controls over explanatory panels.
- Make PDF pages visible when editing or translating.
